# Create requirements

Follow the approved request, applicable project conventions, and the user's
approval, glossary, privacy, and reporting rules. This skill does not authorize
file changes, commands, or changes to the approved scope.

## Method

Apply requirements engineering principles, including SMART (specific,
measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound) and applicable INCOSE
(International Council on Systems Engineering) methods and guidance.

1. Identify the requirement level, source, intended outcome, and relevant
   constraints. Keep product needs distinct from software design choices.
2. Give each requirement a stable identifier and a single, clear obligation.
   Record its source and links to applicable parent requirements or models.
3. Make the requirement necessary, unambiguous, singular, feasible, verifiable,
   and traceable. Apply SMART in context. Include timing criteria where
   applicable; do not invent deadlines, acceptance thresholds, or feasibility
   evidence.
4. Define the conditions, measurable result, and verification method. Use
   existing project formats. Mark missing decisions and evidence explicitly
   rather than presenting assumptions as approved requirements.
5. Review the requirement set for consistency and completeness within the
   approved scope. Identify conflicts, duplication, and missing coverage.
6. Identify the guidance used and any unverified criteria. Do not claim formal
   INCOSE compliance or approval without evidence. If authoritative guidance is
   unavailable, state which method-specific checks could not be completed.

## Result

Use the project's established format, or return requirements in chat with their
identifiers, statements, sources, conditions, verification criteria, and approval
status. Keep proposals separate from approved requirements. Report unresolved
questions and quality checks as pass, fail, or not checked, with evidence or
the reason. Preserve the role agent's product models or software design and
technical specification; this shared method does not replace those outputs.
