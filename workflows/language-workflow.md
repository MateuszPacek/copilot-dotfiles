# Language workflow

Read for explicitly requested or required ASD-STE100 writing, rewriting, language review, or rule explanations.

Ordinary chat, baseline wording, and planning use concise, clear language without mandatory standard, dictionary, or glossary checks solely for wording. Mentioning the standard or reviewing workflow rules alone does not require this procedure. If the task explicitly requires its output to follow the standard, apply all relevant checks below.

## Sources and meaning

Use the bundled references in the `asd-ste100` skill when available and
permitted. They are an unofficial, incomplete aid, not the authority. A local
or uploaded copy of the standard is not required for this workflow.
Consult accepted glossaries locally before drafting when applicable.
Read its relevant procedure sections and references before each phase unless their current contents are already available; do not load unrelated background.
Check applicable writing rules, each content word's part of speech, and its meaning in context, not spelling alone.
If a needed entry or rule is absent, unreadable, or ambiguous, mark it internally as not checked with the specific cause. Do not infer a ruling from memory or another source.
Do not replace an unavailable skill or reference with a compliance claim.

Preserve facts, objects, qualifiers, conditions, and obligations. Never change meaning to fit vocabulary.
Keep code, commands, paths, and exact quotes unchanged.
When baseline wording must follow the standard, prepare it with these checks before requesting approval. Assess standard-based output against the approved version, not a later rewrite.
Keep glossaries and confidential text local; do not send them to an unapproved service.

## Glossaries

Read `~/.copilot/glossary.md` and the current project's glossary before using a new term.
Keep general terms in the personal glossary; keep company and project terms in the project's glossary, not personal files.
Present each new term as a proposal. Ask the user to approve, change, or reject it before treating it as accepted or adding it.
Use only words and meanings supported by the available skill references or accepted glossary in definitions.
Do not define a term through itself or through circular definitions.
If no permitted definition is possible, definitions conflict, or a necessary glossary cannot be read, stop and explain the substantive issue.
User approval does not make a term part of ASD-STE100.

## Reporting

For routine work, keep findings, unresolved words, and incomplete-check causes internal. Do not append language-check status, disclaimers, or compliance claims to responses or documents.
Do not create a separate findings file unless requested or necessary for the existing task record.
For an explicitly requested language review, return relevant violations, part of speech, source rulings, proposed corrections, and specific unchecked items with causes.
Do not promise full compliance from a word list or simple wording. Human approval remains necessary for explicitly reviewed text.
Keep approval questions, actual task failures, and substantive blockers visible; internal language reporting never authorizes altered facts or fabricated results.
